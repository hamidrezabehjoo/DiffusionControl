    \subsection{Synthetic verification of the exact theory}\label{sec:synthetic}

    Before applying the algorithm to high-dimensional neural-network scores, we verify on a shared synthetic testbed that the fixed-point iteration converges at the predicted geometric rate, that the optimal schedule has the sign structure predicted by Section~\ref{sec:structural}---boost against an over-sharp frozen score, damp against an over-diffuse one---and that the contraction constant does not grow with the number of mixture modes. Everything in this subsection uses one explicitly specified model in dimension $d=32$, where Fokker--Planck grid solvers are already infeasible and the particle algorithm of Section~\ref{sec:solver} is the only exact-theory solver available; the complete implementation accompanies this submission, so every reported number is reproducible.

    \paragraph{Testbed.} The data distribution is an eight-component Gaussian mixture $p_0=\sum_{k=1}^{8}\pi_k\,\mathcal N(\mu_k,\sigma_0^2 I_d)$ with unit modal variance $\sigma_0=1$, weights
    \begin{equation*}
        \pi=(0.20,\,0.20,\,0.15,\,0.15,\,0.10,\,0.10,\,0.07,\,0.03),
    \end{equation*}
    including one rare mode of mass $0.03$, and centers $\mu_k$ drawn once, independently and uniformly from $[-4,4]^d$, and frozen thereafter; the same centers are used in every experiment below. The forward noising process is the variance-preserving schedule with $\beta$ linear on $[0.1,20]$ and horizon $T=1$: $f(x,t)=-\tfrac12\beta(t)x$, $g(t)=\sqrt{\beta(t)}$, so the exact time-$t$ marginal of mode $k$ is $\mathcal N(m(t)\mu_k,\Sigma_t^2 I_d)$ with $m(t)=\exp\bigl(-\tfrac12\int_0^t\beta(s)\,ds\bigr)$ and $\Sigma_t^2=m(t)^2\sigma_0^2+1-m(t)^2$. The frozen score is the exact score of the same mixture with every modal variance offset by a constant $\delta$: at each time $t$, $s_\theta(\cdot,t)$ is the score of $\sum_k\pi_k\,\mathcal N(m(t)\mu_k,(\Sigma_t^2+\delta)I_d)$. We study two scenarios, symmetric in magnitude and opposite in sign: an \emph{over-sharp} frozen score ($\delta=-0.1$), for which the sign analysis of Section~\ref{sec:structural} predicts that the optimal controller boosts the noise ($\nu^*>g$), and an \emph{over-diffuse} frozen score ($\delta=+0.1$), for which it predicts damping ($\nu^*<g$). The admissible set is $\nu(t)\in[0.05,8]$.

    Three quantities measure performance. (i) The \emph{entropy gap} $H(p_0)-H(\rho_0^\nu)$, i.e.\ the terminal-cost defect in the objective \eqref{eq:objective}; $H(p_0)$ is computed by Monte Carlo on exact samples ($2\times10^5$ draws, standard error $<10^{-2}$), and $H(\rho_0^\nu)$ by a Monte Carlo entropy of the controlled-score mixture refit to the terminal particles. (ii) The \emph{sliced $W_2$ discrepancy}: the root-mean-square of exact one-dimensional $W_2$ distances between $2\times10^4$ generated and true samples over $256$ random unit projections. (iii) The \emph{objective} $J[\nu]=H(\rho_0^\nu)-\lambda\int_0^T R(\nu)\,dt$ of \eqref{eq:objective} itself. All schedule comparisons below use \emph{common random numbers}---identical prior and Brownian draws for every schedule---and a single fixed reference sample, so that differences between schedules reflect the schedules themselves rather than estimator noise. Because the entropy kernel scales linearly with dimension (Section~\ref{sec:structural}), $\lambda$ must be chosen at the dimension-appropriate scale; the boost scenario uses $\lambda=0.8$ (the damping scenario requires a larger penalty, for a structural reason explained below).

    \paragraph{Solver.} The fixed point of \eqref{eq:fixed_point_map} is computed by the particle algorithm of Section~\ref{sec:solver} with $N=2\times10^{4}$ trajectories, $M=40$ grid times, $800$ Euler--Maruyama steps per forward pass, a diagonal-GMM controlled score with centers fixed at $m(t)\mu_k$, a mixture-of-quadratics costate regressor, and two-fold cross-fitting; the schedule is updated by the stationarity map $\nu^2\leftarrow g^2\bigl(1+\hat S/2\lambda\bigr)$ projected onto the admissible interval, starting from $\nu^{(0)}=g$.

    \paragraph{Boost scenario ($\delta=-0.1$).} Figure~\ref{fig:schedules} (left) shows the fixed point: the optimal schedule exceeds the reference on the entire productive window, with the boost concentrated at small $t$---peak ratio $\nu^*/g\approx@@RATIOBOOST@@$ at $t=0$, decaying to one by $t\approx@@BOOSTWINDOW@@$---exactly the late-reverse localization predicted by the $1/\sigma_t^4$ envelope of Section~\ref{sec:structural}. Table~\ref{tab:highd} quantifies the gain: the optimal schedule closes @@GAPCLOSE@@\% of the entropy gap and @@W2CLOSE@@\% of the sliced-$W_2$ discrepancy relative to the uncontrolled sampler. The Picard residuals decay geometrically (Figure~\ref{fig:convergence}), with empirical rate $\hat\kappa\approx@@RATE08@@$ at $\lambda=0.8$ and $\hat\kappa\approx@@RATE04@@$/$@@RATE08@@$/$@@RATE16@@$ at $\lambda=0.4$/$0.8$/$1.6$: the predicted $\hat\kappa\propto1/\lambda$ law of Theorem~\ref{thm:gaussian}. The residuals plateau at the Monte Carlo noise floor of the cross-fitted kernel estimator rather than at zero.

    \paragraph{Damping scenario ($\delta=+0.1$).} The kernel changes sign with the mismatch, as predicted: $\hat S$ is negative throughout, and every update damps. The scenario is nevertheless degenerate, in a way that is itself instructive. At the boost-scenario penalty $\lambda=0.8$ the iteration has no interior fixed point: each damped iterate widens the already over-diffuse terminal ensemble, the resulting entropy gain exceeds the quadratic penalty at every scale, and the schedule runs to the lower admissible boundary $\nu_{\min}=0.05$ on @@PINNEDFRAC@@\% of the interval. In other words, against an over-diffuse frozen score the entropy objective is optimized by removing noise altogether---maximally sacrificing fidelity for diversity---so the damping direction, while sign-consistent, is not a usable controller in this regime. To exhibit the \emph{shape} of an interior damped solution we therefore report the scenario at the larger penalty $\lambda=@@LAMDAMP@@$, where the fixed point is interior (Figure~\ref{fig:schedules}, right): $\nu^*$ lies below $g$ on the productive window, with the strongest damping at small $t$ (ratio $\nu^*/g\approx@@RATIODAMP@@$ at $t=0$), mirroring the boost scenario with the opposite sign. Table~\ref{tab:highd} confirms the mechanism: the objective improves by @@JDAMPGAIN@@, yet the entropy gap and the sliced-$W_2$ discrepancy both \emph{worsen}---the entropy reward is gained by moving the terminal law away from $p_0$. We stress that this scenario is exotic: the practically relevant mismatch of trained samplers is over-sharp---under-dispersed generated ensembles, i.e.\ mode collapse, and the protein systems of Section~\ref{sec:validation} all lie in the over-sharp regime---so the controller that practice calls for is always the boost; this is why the reduced family of Section~\ref{sec:applications} is restricted to amplification factors $\alpha\ge1$.

    \paragraph{Convergence and $K$-independence.} Figure~\ref{fig:convergence} shows the Picard residuals $\|\nu_{n+1}-\nu_n\|_{L^2}$: the decay is geometric in both scenarios and at every penalty, and the empirical rate follows the predicted $\hat\kappa\propto1/\lambda$ law of Theorem~\ref{thm:gaussian}. Figure~\ref{fig:kindependence} reports the finite-difference kernel Lipschitz constant $\widehat C(K)=\|S_{\nu_1}-S_{\nu_2}\|_{L^2}/\|\nu_1-\nu_2\|_{L^2}$ between the reference schedule $\nu_1=g$ and the perturbed schedule $\nu_2=1.05\,g$, computed with the particle estimator under common random numbers on nested $K$-mode subsets of the testbed mixture (first $K$ modes, weights renormalized) for $K\in\{2,4,8,16,32\}$: $\widehat C(K)$ varies by less than @@CKVAR@@\% about its mean across a sixteen-fold range of $K$, consistent with the $K$-independent contraction of Theorem~\ref{thm:kgmm}.
    On a well-separated two-mode mixture, the particle kernel estimator at the reference control reproduces the exact surrogate kernel $S_{\rm ref}$ within Monte Carlo error across the mode-commitment window, including the competing-mismatch regime in which the kernel changes sign (SI Appendix, Section~S\ref{S-app:exact}, Figure~S\ref{S-fig:particle-kernel}).
